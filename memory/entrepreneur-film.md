# Entrepreneur film — piece 2 blueprint

Studio ads for **us**. Not a client social-media service. Not small-business new. Not refresh. Gate: **plan only**. Do not generate stills or video from this file. Ewan marks frames and prompts before any model is called.

Locked pitch: a **web space** they can actually use — a site they can send, a sandbox while they are still working. Kin to press kits, different style. Can be a sandbox, a form, a walk-through — not only a public homepage. Voice is **dialogue**: spoken, finishes the thought. Not a poster. Not “a room an investor can sit in.”

Public word for the working copy is **sandbox**. Host package is live site + constant sandbox. You leave a note, we take it into a human-led build, you choose when to push. Ewan’s public word is **builder**. Ads say **you**. Design Lab North **are**. UK English. No agency-speak, no hype, no riddles. Do not print amounts. Do not mention how sites are made internally. Do not invent awards, clients, or where the house lives.

Target finished films: **15–20 seconds**. The existing host loop (`Site/public/brief/social-loop/loop.html`, ~8.2s cycle, live blip at 5.55s) is a **3–4 second chunk** in this film — about a quarter — maybe sped up. Picture only in the frame. Voice and captions later, recorded onto a silent master.

This is not a posting calendar.

## 1. Who this film is for (real groups, not persona nicknames)

Do not aim this film at “everyday entrepreneurs” in the GEM sense. GEM UK 2024/25 is clear that high-growth firms are a tiny slice of people who start businesses. Our door is people who need a **web space another person can actually use** — an investor, an angel, a grant panel, a corporate partner, a serious first customer who is doing diligence. That is a subset of founders, not the mass of sole traders. On a social video they are not “in a room.” They are on a phone. The next line has to exist.

### 1.1 The groups that actually match the locked pitch

**A. First-time equity raisers, often still pre-seed or seed.**  
Beauhurst / Penningtons (State of UK First-time Investment 2026): **2,730** UK companies raised a first equity round in 2025 (+29.9% on 2024), the highest count since 2021. Together they raised £4.4bn. The **median first round fell 20% to £200k**; H1 2026 median fell further to **£170k**. More first-time deals completed **outside London than inside it** for the first time since 2022 (outside London +33.2%, London +27.2%). West Midlands nearly doubled (+95.9%), Northern Ireland +72.7%, Scotland +59.7%. Manchester had **46** first-time deals in the last 12 months — the largest count of any area outside London. Sources: [Beauhurst report page](https://www.beauhurst.com/research/state-of-uk-first-time-investment-2026/), [PDF](https://beauhurst-assets.fly.storage.tigris.dev/blog-files/State%20of%20UK%20First-time%20deal%20Report%202026.pdf), [Business Quarter summary](https://businessquarter.co.uk/first-time-investment-spreads-further-beyond-london/).  
Film implication: they are raising **small first cheques**, often from several people, and they need a space that looks held together **before** the meeting, not a brochure after they have money.

**B. SEIS / EIS founders talking to UK angels.**  
HMRC SEIS 2024–25: **2,430** companies raised £276m (+14%); **1,775** of those were raising under SEIS for the first time (£229m). Most companies received over £50k (66%); 45% raised over £100k; 32% raised over £150k after the 2023 cap rose to £250k. FounderCatalyst (Jan 2025–Mar 2026 platform data): SEIS is used in **92%** of rounds up to £250k; median angel ticket ~£10k, average ~£23k — rounds are **built from many small commitments**. SeedLegals Termometer: **85%** of companies on their platform either have SEIS/EIS advance assurance or intend to; founders put a median **£26k of their own money** in before the first round; “nobody invests on just a PowerPoint slide.” Sources: [HMRC SEIS 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/enterprise-investment-scheme-and-seed-enterprise-investment-scheme-may-2026/enterprise-investment-scheme-and-seed-enterprise-investment-scheme-2026), [FounderCatalyst](https://www.foundercatalyst.com/blog/key-insights-from-initial-funding-rounds-january-2025-march-2026), [SeedLegals Termometer](https://seedlegals.com/termometer/).  
Film implication: the viewer is assembling a **space several people can actually use**, not launching a consumer shop. The walk-through / press-kit kin is the right *object*. The *speech* is: they will look you up.

**C. Solo founders who still have to look like a going concern.**  
UK VC-backed companies are unusually solo: one 2025–26 compilation put **solo founding at 38–54%** of UK VC-funded startups (US 15–20%), climbing toward **54% in 2025**. SeedLegals’ own cut: solo founders are disadvantaged once a priced round opens; many raise a small instrument first, then add a co-founder. Sources: [SeedLegals on solo founders](https://seedlegals.com/resources/is-it-really-harder-for-solo-founders-to-raise-investment/).  
Film implication: the fear is not “I need a shop front.” The fear is “I look like one person with a Notion page.” A designed **space** (not a template site) is the product.

**D. Founders outside London, especially the North.**  
London still takes the **larger amounts** (Beauhurst: London ~50% of UK equity deals and ~62% of value in 2025 overall; HMRC: London + South East ~66% of SEIS **money**). Count of **first** deals has tipped outside the capital. North West 2025: **327** equity deals, down 13.7% in count, value up to **£1.63bn** — fewer, larger later deals, which makes the *first* room even more load-bearing. Manchester is the non-London city that already compounds. NatWest Accelerator hubs include **Manchester, Leeds, Newcastle** (and London). PraeSeed / NPIF II 2025 cohort: Lancashire, Yorkshire, Merseyside, Greater Manchester, Durham, Cumbria, Tyne and Wear, with ~£200k tickets. Baltic Ventures (Liverpool) ran a 2025 tech cohort. Sources: [TheBusinessDesk North West](https://www.thebusinessdesk.com/northwest/news/2167211-nw-equity-investment-tops-1.6bn-as-investors-focus-on-fewer-but-larger-deals), [NatWest Accelerator](https://www.natwest.com/business/business-services/entrepreneur-accelerator.html), [PraeSeed](https://www.pxnventures.co.uk/second-ever-npif-ii-backed-praeseed-cohort-revealed/), [Baltic Ventures / Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/news/baltic-ventures-pumps-300k-into-2025-tech-startup-cohort/).  
Film implication: the film can be **North without saying where we live**. Do not perform “Northern agency.” Do not chase only London SaaS.

**E. Women raising, or trying to.**  
GEM UK 2024/25: women’s TEA **10%** (from ~3.5% in 2002); female-to-male TEA ratio **69%**. Equity access for women-led firms remains the stuck problem. Beauhurst / Idea London: all-female teams ~7% of 2025 UK equity deals and ~2% of value; teams with at least one woman ~25% of deals / 15% of value. HMRC: women ~19% of EIS claims and ~18% of SEIS claims (2022–23 to 2024–25), ~14% of value. Sources: [GEM UK 2024/25 PDF](https://www.enterpriseresearch.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/GEM-UK-24-25.pdf), [GEM consortium news](https://gemconsortium.org/news/record-high-uk-entrepreneurship-driven-by-women%2C-yet-hurdles-remain), [Idea London on 2025 deals](https://idea-london.co.uk/uk-pre-seed-funding-data/).  
Film implication: do not gender the voice. Do not “empower.” Do make a room that does not look like a boys’-club pitch theatre or a pastel lifestyle reel.

**F. Age bands — who is actually raising, vs who is starting.**  
GEM: average age of a UK early-stage entrepreneur in 2024 is **39** (Scotland 37). TEA for **18–29** rose to nearly **16%** (from ~5% pre-GFC); the age mix of all early-stage entrepreneurs has shifted from early 40s toward early 30s since 2019. Beauhurst high-growth founders: **average 36 at incorporation**, modal band **30–39** (39.5% in London, 35.1% in the Midlands); unicorn founders average 34; over-50s are 11.5% UK-wide, 9% in London. Sources: [GEM Scotland 24/25](https://www.enterpriseresearch.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/GEM-scotland-24-25-1.pdf), [Beauhurst founder age](https://www.beauhurst.com/blog/age-uk-startup-founders/).  
Film implication: the **investor-attracting** viewer is mostly **late 20s to early 40s**, with a real tail of 45–55. Do not make a TikTok-teen film. Do not make a grey-temple corporate film.

### 1.2 Who this film is not for (keep the doors unmixed)

- A local shop that needs a first site. That is **piece 3**.
- A firm with a live site they are ashamed of, or not. That is **piece 4**.
- Journalists hunting a newsroom. We already have press kits (`/epk`). This film is kin to that object, not a journalist product ad.
- People buying a social-media retainer. We already pass that work.
- The mass of UK businesses: 5.7 million private-sector firms at start 2025; **75%** have no employees besides the owners; **57%** are sole proprietorships. Construction, professional/scientific/technical, and wholesale/retail dominate counts. Source: [BPE 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release). Those people are not this door.

### 1.3 Sectors — what to show, what not to fake

First-time equity **value** in 2025–H1 2026 is skewed to **AI** (Beauhurst: AI 58.6% of first-time **value** in H1 2026, one large round doing a lot of the work). Deal **counts** still sit in application software, digital technology, professional and business services, financial services, plus thinner life-sciences tickets. SEIS angels still back **all kinds of businesses**; the median first round is not an AI mega-round.

Do **not** put a fake AI dashboard, a fake fintech app, or a named vertical in the film. The schematic rule from piece 1 still holds: if the window holds a photoreal mock-up, a model invents a generic office. For this door the inner object is **story plates, a walk, a form, a room** — not a pretend product UI.

### 1.4 What “attracting investment” means in the picture

Investors Google you before or after a pitch. UK and adjacent practitioner writing in 2024–26 is consistent: the site (or the space they land on) is checked for **credibility in the first thirty seconds to a minute**, on a phone, often between meetings. They look for a clear offer, named people, some proof, a way in. A crumpled or template presence is treated as an execution proxy — “can this team ship?” Sources: [Webshape (UK)](https://webshapedesign.co.uk/blog/why-your-website-matters-during-a-funding-round/), [Fundraising101 Academy, Scotland](https://fundraising101.academy/raise-ready-digital-presence-review/), [Fourmeta](https://www.fourmeta.com/blog/your-website-is-your-pitch-deck-building-investor-trust-online), [HoM](https://www.homwebdev.com/website-for-investors/), [Plinth checklist](https://plinthstudio.dev/blog/founder-website-checklist).

Rise PR (Nov 2025) audited **334** UK startups that had already raised seed or pre-seed in 2025: only **13.2%** had a press or news section; **0.9%** a named media contact; **<2%** downloadable assets; **4.2%** a boilerplate. Source: [Rise PR](https://risepr.co.uk/blog/only-one-in-eight-funded-uk-startups-have-a-press-or-news-page/). So the **press-kit-shaped space** is still rare. That is our opening: not “you need a website,” but “you need a room people can sit in, and almost nobody has built you one.”

Distinguish three objects we already know, and do not mash them in the film:

| Object | Who it is for | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Public site | Customers, staff, the world | Live presence |
| Press kit / EPK | Journalists, sometimes partners | Story, assets, a walk |
| Data room | Investors under NDA | Documents, cap table, diligence |

Our film is **none of those three as a product name**. It is the **space between them**: a designed room you can walk an investor through **before** a data room, **without** pretending it is a shop. GOV.UK’s own investment-readiness note treats a clean data room as a signal of discipline; that is later. Source: [GOV.UK data room checklist](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unlocking-space-for-investment-growth-hub/track-1-data-room-essentials-documents-checklist). We do not say “data room” on the film.

## 2. What they already believe; what we never say

### 2.1 Beliefs we can sit next to (do not lecture)

- **The deck is not enough.** SeedLegals: people have already put their own money in; investors expect a prototype or a presence, not slides alone.
- **Someone will search us tonight.** The first look is a phone in a quiet or semi-public place. Mute. Short.
- **Template looks like template.** Squarespace / Wix / “we’ll do it in Webflow this weekend” reads as unfinished or as no taste. Founders already know this; they are embarrassed, not ignorant.
- **Agencies will reskin a playbook.** Early-stage fear of agencies is specific: generic solutions, retainers, not understanding the idea, cookie-cutter content. Sources: [Beanfluence on founder fear](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/beanfluencemedia_startups-marketing-marketingagency-activity-7426172978595311617-aLHD), [Sprintli on templated playbooks](https://www.sprintli.com/insights/why-your-startup-keeps-outgrowing-its-marketing-agency). They will skip anything that *looks like an agency reel*.
- **I cannot look unfinished to an investor, but I also cannot look finished-and-fake.** Over-polished stock, fake traction, anonymous “our team,” coming-soon pages, and `.webflow.io` subdomains are all named as red flags in the practitioner lists above.
- **A public marketing site and an investor conversation are different jobs.** They already feel that. The locked pitch honours it: the thing can be a sandbox, a form, a walk-through.

### 2.2 What we never say (film, captions, comments, thumbnail)

House locks:

- How the work is made internally. No tools, no desk, no “AI-built.” Ewan is **builder**.
- Amounts, retainers, weekly host figures. Research money stays in this file. Not on the film.
- Client names, plot names, greenhouse product names. No ModYu, no Swarm, no Choozlist, no Various Titles, no DAA, no PFP.
- Invented awards. Dave’s locked practice copy is not a 15-second CV.
- Where the house lives. No city-as-credential.
- “Iterative.” The three notes *are* the engagement; do not print the word.
- Agency-speak: craft, journey, unlock, elevate, disrupt, ecosystem (public), synergy, “we’re passionate.”
- Hype. Superlatives. “World-class.” “Unique system” as a boast.
- Riddles. If they have to decode it, it is wrong.
- “They.” Ads say **you**.
- Slighting a live site or the people who built it (refresh door owns this; this door must not borrow it).
- “We build websites” as the **lead**. That is true of Build, and it is the wrong door here.
- “System” as a dump on a founder. Do not stack “methodology / 8-phase / proprietary.” If a word only makes sense to the desk, it is not in the ad.
- CMS language: pages, modules, drag-and-drop, preview, staging, publish queue.
- “Press kit” as the product name on this film. Kin, different style. Journalists have `/epk`. This viewer is raising or attracting money.
- A posting calendar, a “content engine,” “we’ll run your socials.”
- Logo bumper, showreel montage, sliding client logos, stock office, stock handshake, stock skyline.
- Keyboard clacks, music swell, in-frame film line (clips on social — already true of piece 1).

### 2.3 Language that works on this door

Use, in this order of safety:

- **They're going to look you up** — the opener. Completes in the next line.
- **Space** — a web space they can use. Always attach the site. Never hang it.
- **Site** — what they send. Ordinary word.
- **Deck** — what is not enough, said without mockery.
- **Sandbox** — while you're still working; public word for the working copy.
- **Note** — you leave it; we build.
- **Push / you say when** — you choose.
- **Keep going / iterate** — only as: without taking the live site down. Never as a chip.

Use sparingly:

- **Form** — if the picture is a form becoming the space; not “fill in our brief.”
- **Walk-through** — pages they can actually move through.

Do not use on this door:

- Shop, brochure, website package, CMS, preview, staging, brand refresh, facelift, unique to you (piece 3 owns uniqueness), market/conflict/competitor (piece 4).

Campus already has a Strategy sitting that talks to this person without a pitch-deck look: Start-up strategy is two hours — who you want to be, how you show it, who it is for. The film must not sell that sitting as a product. It can *feel* like the same seriousness.

## 3. Channels and format (15–20s, mute-first, captions)

This section is **where the film must work**, not a calendar of posts.

### 3.1 Where this person actually watches short video

**LinkedIn is the primary surface.**  
The viewer (founder, angel, associate, accelerator manager) is on LinkedIn for work. LinkedIn video viewership +36% YoY (Oct 2024–Jan 2025); video creation growing at 2× other formats; dedicated **vertical feed** from March 2024. LinkedIn’s own B2B guidance: video ads **15–30 seconds** for top-of-funnel; hook in the **first six seconds**; **no intro boards / bumpers**; **92% of mobile video watched without sound** (their cited figure); 9:16 showing a **24% CTR lift**; selfie-style +11% engagement — useful for an optional Ewan cut, not a mandate. Videos **autoplay muted** in feed and ads. Native upload, not a YouTube link. Sources: [LinkedIn: 13 tips](https://www.linkedin.com/business/marketing/blog/content-marketing/13-top-tips-for-compelling-b2b-video-content-on-linkedin), [ContentGrip on LinkedIn video 2025](https://www.contentgrip.com/linkedin-video-growth/), [Brandwatch LinkedIn specs](https://www.brandwatch.com/blog/linkedin-video-specs/).  
DataReportal Digital 2025 UK: LinkedIn ads claim **45.0 million** UK members (registered, not MAU — not comparable to TikTok MAU). 45% female / 55% male ad audience. Source: [DataReportal UK 2025](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-united-kingdom).

**Instagram Reels is the second surface, same picture.**  
Ofcom Online Nation 2025: YouTube 94% of UK online adults, **51 min/day**; Instagram **20 min/day**, strong **18–34** skew; TikTok **56%** of online adults, 18–34 at **49 min/day**. Facebook/Messenger 93% but weighted **35+** — not the raiser’s first feed. Source: [Ofcom Online Nation 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/online-research/online-nation/2025/online-nations-report-2025.pdf).  
Instagram Reels: mixed mute. Instagram’s CEO has said ~**50%** watch Reels muted (widely cited 2025 creator commentary). Feed placement is more mute than the Reels tab. Same 9:16 master as LinkedIn. Do not add Reels-only trends, audio memes, or stickers.

**YouTube Shorts is reach, not the conversation.**  
YouTube is the UK’s top social/VSP by users and time. Shorts are sound-on more often than LinkedIn. Our film is mute-first graphic; it will still play, but it will not “feel native” to Shorts entertainment. Use the same 9:16 if we put it there. Do not recut with music.

**TikTok is optional and late.**  
Ofcom: 56% of online adults, young. Sound-on culture (~80% in industry round-ups). A silent Paper/Ink graphic with a lecture voice will look like an ad that wandered in. If we ever run it, it is the **same silent picture** plus captions — we do not invent a TikTok personality. Founder-marketing advice in 2025 still splits: LinkedIn rewards first-person business insight; TikTok rewards idea-led raw talk and punishes “about us.” Source pattern: [Max Nimmo on platform split](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/max-nimmo_i-got-1-million-views-on-a-marketing-tiktok-activity-7375823718058496000-aada).

**X is not a pillar.** Ofcom: 39% of online adults in May 2025, down from 45%.

### 3.2 What they skip (design the cut against this)

On LinkedIn / Reels, this viewer skips:

1. **Logo sting, then music, then the idea.** LinkedIn: intro boards eat the six-second window.
2. **Agency reels.** Client montage, sliding marks, “strategy / design / digital” tricolour, stock office, drone of a city, staff laughing at a stand-up. Reads as “this is not for me, this is their new-business tape.”
3. **Website-builder ads.** Before/after homepage, cursor painting a hero, “go live in a day.” That is the small-business-new confusion we must not import.
4. **Talking head that starts with the name of the firm.** If Ewan is on camera, the **first line is the viewer’s problem**, not “Hi, I’m…”
5. **Captions that clip.** Already why piece 1 has no in-frame film line. Burned-in captions sit in a **safe lower-third band** or as a few large words, never a paragraph across the chamfer.
6. **Sound-dependent jokes, whooshes, typing sounds.** LinkedIn is work-hours mute. Digiday’s old but stubborn Facebook figure: ~**85%** of publisher video views without sound; HubSpot’s own Facebook test: ~**95%** silent until they captioned, then 60–70%. Sources: [Digiday 2016](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/), [HubSpot experiment](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/hubspot-social-media-experiment). Storykit’s LinkedIn member survey: **56%** desktop usually sound on vs **37%** mobile — so even LinkedIn is not 100% mute, but **mobile founders between meetings are**. Source: [Storykit](https://storykit.io/blog/social-media-videos-does-sound-matter).
7. **A call to “book a chemistry call” before they know what the object is.** End is a room, a note, a door. Not a funnel.

- **Reels / Shorts:** 15s is the more native skim; 20s if the walk-through earns it.
- Do not make a 60s “explainer.” This door is one idea.

## 4. How the SAME sandbox→live mechanic is communicated *differently* here (not a site CMS demo)

Everyone on every door still sees: **live + constant sandbox**, you leave a **note**, we take it into a **human-led build**, **you** choose when to **push**. The **lead** changes.

### 4.1 What piece 1 currently says (do not repeat as the whole film)

Piece 1 (`loop.html`): two chamfered **windows**, Sandbox left / top, Live right / bottom, three notes type and cement in colour, Push, live **blips**. Cycle **8.2s**. Kickers on the rooms. It reads as **how hosting works** — a pair of site skins. Correct for host. Wrong as the *opening* of an investor-space film. If this is the whole 15s, they will file us under “web agency with a clever CMS.”

### 4.2 What this door must make the mechanic mean

Here the two rooms are not “draft website / live website.” They are:

- **Sandbox = the room while you talk.** The investor is not invited into the working copy. You and we are. Notes are conversation, not tickets in a backlog screenshot.
- **Live = the presence you show.** The thing a stranger, an angel, a panel can sit in when you say go.
- **Push = you choose when the presence updates.** Control. Not a deploy log.

So the same clunks (note → human build → you push → live matches) now mean: **you can have a serious presence without freezing the idea, and without showing the workshop.**

### 4.3 Picture translation (mechanic → this door)

| Mechanic (all doors) | Piece 1 picture | Piece 2 picture (this film) |
|---|---|---|
| Two states | Two site windows | One **space** with two states, *or* a room and a door |
| Note | Grey bar types in the sandbox skin | A note left **in the room** (card, line, spoken-to-the-builder) — still schematic |
| We build | Implied by delay then blip | Voice: “We build.” / “We take it in.” Picture: the space does **not** magically DIY. Optional: Ewan as builder, not as presenter of a CMS |
| Push | Chamfered Push chip between windows | Push as **you** opening the door / agreeing the presence. Keep the word **Push** — it is already studio language |
| Live blip | Site skin matches | The **presence** matches: plates, walk, form — not nav + hero bars |

### 4.4 What not to demo

- No mouse cursor dragging components.
- No URL bar, no `localhost`, no dashboard, no “save.”
- No before/after homepage.
- No fake metrics, fake ARR, fake logos of investors.
- No PDF-in-a-browser (that is a data room).
- No HT4 / MAP chrome from our own kits. Kin, **different style**.

### 4.5 Where the existing GIF/HTML sits

It is the **proof of the package**, not the story of the space.

- First three-quarters: new picture (space / walk / form).
- Last quarter: sped-up host loop as “and the room stays a sandbox while you talk; live when you say go.”
- Alternative (test): loop as a **picture-in-picture** inside the room’s end wall — still schematic, still Paper/Ink — so it is *in* the space, not a smash-cut to a different ad.

If the loop is used full-bleed at the end, **drop the kicker “Design / Build → Host”** for this door. That kicker is campus catalogue. Replace with nothing, or with **Sandbox** / **Live** only.

## 5. Film architecture: second-by-second 15s and 20s cuts

Picture has **no** baked film line. Italics below are **voice later**. Captions are a separate pass (section 7).

Existing loop facts to honour: idle → caret/type/cement ×3 → Push at 5.1s of the 8.2s cycle → live blip at 5.55s. For this film, **compress that cycle to ~3.5–4.0s** (about 2×). Do not redesign the clunks. Do not bring back the swipe.

### 5.1 What to draw that is **not** the current two-window loop

Four new graphic objects. All Paper/Ink, Aktiv Grotesk, 45° chamfer, no pills/shadows/orbs. Schematic, not photoreal.

1. **The web space.** Site-shaped frames, Paper/Ink, chamfer. Pages they could send. Not an empty architectural room with a slogan. Not a laptop. Not a city.
2. **The walk.** Three **pages** in a line: **Story → Proof → Presence**. We pass through a space people use; we do not swipe a carousel of generic websites.
3. **The form that is a conversation.** Three fields only, in our type: *the idea / who it is for / what they walk*. Submit is not a pill; it is a chamfered **enter**. The form **becomes** the space (the fields lift into pages).
4. **Sandbox beside live.** Closed / working copy vs what they can send. Push is the decision — graphic, not a stock hand. The host loop already draws this; new furniture must not fight it.

**Do not draw:** two CMS windows as the opening shot; a laptop mockup; a phone in a hand; a city; a WeWork; a whiteboard brainstorm; a pitch-deck 16:9 slide with bullet points; a stock “investor.”

### 5.2 20-second master (preferred)

## 6. Picture variants (3–5)

Honour chamfer / Paper / Ink on anything graphic. Never photoreal generic office invented by a model. First GenerateImage pair for piece 1 did that (beige, rounded, monitors) and was thrown away. Same ban here.

### Variant A — Graphic only (default, make first)

Full Paper ground. The room, the walk, the note, the sped-up loop, the door, the mark. No people, or people as **blocks** (shoulders and a head rectangle), never faces. Safest against the office problem. Best mute-first. Best for 9:16 and 16:9 from one drawing system (HTML/CSS like piece 1, then GIF).

**When it wins:** LinkedIn ads; anywhere the agency-reel detector is hot.

**When it loses:** if it is *only* the two windows again, they will not feel a new door.

### Variant B — Graphic walk, no loop until last quarter

Same as A, but the loop is **inset** as a chamfered panel on the back wall of the presence chamber — a picture hanging in the room, then it plays. Makes the mechanic belong to the space.

**When it wins:** if smash-cutting to loop.html feels like two films glued.

### Variant C — Ewan on camera, live-action, graphic end

Ewan, real, in a **real** place we control (plain wall, Paper-like, or Ink-like grey). Tripod. Decent mic. No office set dressing. No branded hoodie unless it already exists and Ewan wants it. He speaks the bed. Cutaways are **our graphics**, not B-roll of keyboards.

LinkedIn’s own note: simple selfie-style +11% engagement; keep it steady (they also say use a tripod). We take the tripod, not the selfie-wobble.

**When it wins:** native LinkedIn posts from a person. Builder, not presenter.

**When it loses:** paid ads that need to look like Design Lab North the studio; or if he does not want to be the face of this door.

### Variant D — Hybrid: Ewan keyed / generated *around* him

Tools may change the background or generate around him. **Optional, not required.**

Rules if we try it:

- He is the only photoreal human.
- Background is **our graphic room** (Paper/Ink, chamfer), not a model’s office, loft, or “startup HQ.”
- Generate the **room stills in house** (HTML, same as piece 1). Key him over those stills / that loop. Do not ask a video model to invent the room.
- No extra generated people. No invented windows-with-city. No plants-as-lifestyle.
- If the key is dirty, **stop**. Variant C is better than a halo.

**When it wins:** one film that is both a person and the space.

**When it loses:** any hint of Uncanny WeWork.

### Variant E — Form-becomes-room (graphic only, alternate hook)

Open on a chamfered **form** (three fields). At 1.5s the fields lift and become the walk. Rest as A.

**When it wins:** if we need to show “not only a public site” in the first second without the word investor (some feeds punish “investor” as finance-spam). Test against A.

### Recommendation

Draw **A** and **E** as HTML cycles (in house, like piece 1). Film **C** only if Ewan wants a take. Attempt **D** only after A’s room stills exist, so the background is ours. **B** is an edit of A, not a new draw.

Do not produce five finished films. Produce **one 20s graphic master (A)** with a **15s cut**, then decide if Ewan’s face earns a second master.
