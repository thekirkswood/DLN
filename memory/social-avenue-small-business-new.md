## 1. Who this film is for

### The job they are in

Someone who has a trade, a room, a limited company, or a practice, and **does not yet have a proper public face**. Facebook page, Checkatrade, Google listing, WhatsApp, a mate’s Wix, a parked domain, or nothing. The film is not for a founder raising money (piece 2) and not for a firm that already has a live site they want looking at (piece 4).

The UK still has a large first-site pool. The Government’s UK Business Data Survey 2024 (fieldwork Oct 2023–Feb 2024, 3,911 businesses) found **68% of UK businesses have a website** — so about **a third do not**. Ownership rises with size:

| Size | Have a website |
|---|---|
| Sole traders | 65% |
| Micro | 74% |
| Small (10–49) | 93% |
| Medium | 97% |
| Large | 99% |

Source: [UK Business Data Survey 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/uk-business-data-survey-2024/uk-business-data-survey-2024). Health / social work and arts / entertainment both sat at 86% — sectors more likely to already have a site. Construction, hospitality and many sole-trader trades sit in the gap below that, even though the survey does not print a clean construction website rate in the narrative.

Nomada Digital’s 2025 read of the same survey (and Google Trends) put the “no website” share at 32%, with freelance website-builder searches up 40% while “website builder” searches fell 33% — a move **away from DIY platforms toward a person**. Treat that as a directional trade press read, not an official series: [Workplace Journal, 30 Sep 2025](https://workplacejournal.co.uk/2025/09/a-third-of-uk-businesses-remain-offline-despite-digital-growth/).

### Legal form and who actually starts

At the start of 2025 the UK private sector was **5.69 million** businesses. **5.64 million** were small (0–49). **4.27 million** employed nobody besides the owner. Legal form: **3.2 million sole proprietorships (57%)**, **2.1 million companies (37%)**, **368,000 partnerships (6%)**. Source: [Business population estimates 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/business-population-estimates-2025/business-population-estimates-for-the-uk-and-regions-2025-statistical-release) (official statistics in development).

New limited companies keep arriving at industrial scale even as incorporations cooled: **801,864 incorporations** in FYE 2025, down 10% on the year before; private limited companies are 92.6% of the register. Q1 2025 still saw **211,112** incorporations. Sources: [Companies register activities FYE 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/companies-register-activities-statistical-release-april-2024-to-march-2025/companies-register-activities-april-2024-to-march-2025); [Incorporated companies Jan–Mar 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-january-to-march-2025/incorporated-companies-in-the-uk-january-to-march-2025).

ONS VAT/PAYE businesses (the registered slice): **2.735 million** in March 2025; companies 76.7%; sole proprietors 13.9% and falling; **44% of companies are single-employee limited companies**, concentrated in professional / scientific / technical and construction. Source: [ONS UK business; activity, size and location 2025](https://www.ons.gov.uk/businessindustryandtrade/business/activitysizeandlocation/bulletins/ukbusinessactivitysizeandlocation/2025).

**Film implication:** two overlapping buyers, not one cartoon “startup”.

1. **Sole trader / unregistered** — often already trading on word of mouth, Facebook, or a directory. First *proper* brand and first site. Pays from the same pot as van, kit, and rent. Highest “no website” rate (35% of sole traders).
2. **New limited company** — just incorporated, or trading as Ltd for the first time. Needs a name, a look, and a URL that is not `something.wixsite.com`. Often a one-person company (the 44% single-employee Ltd). Pays as director; may have a spouse/partner on the books.

Do not pitch “start-up nation” or investor rooms. That is piece 2.

## 2. Beliefs / never-say list

### Beliefs we are asking them to hold after 15–20 seconds

1. **We start with your brand.** The site is not a theme with a logo dropped in.
2. **What we make is yours.** Unique to them. Not a template.
3. **You get a live site and a sandbox beside it.** One package. They can talk to it all day.
4. **You leave a note. We take it into a human-led build.** Not a self-serve editor. Not a bot.
5. **You say when it goes live.** Empty until the first Push. Then it can grow, and they still choose the next Push.
6. **Design Lab North are people.** Designer and builder. North, plain, not a London deck.

### Decision language that already matches this door (use; do not thicken)

UK small-business web copy in 2024–26 already sells **brand first, unique, simple, not a template**. Independents lead with “no templates”, “yours”, “you don’t need it figured out”, “hand it off”. Examples of the *market language*, not people we cite as kin: [Plesner Studio](https://plesner.studio/) (“100% hand-coded, no templates”); [Rachel Griffiths Design](https://www.rachelgriffithsdesign.co.uk/) (“You don’t need to have it figured out”); [Adam Bradford](https://www.adambradford.com/web-design-portfolio/) (“No templates. No one size fits all”). Bytewise’s 2025 note that buyers clock a template look in about two seconds is useful as a fear, not as a slam: [bespoke vs theme](https://byte-wise.uk/services/bespoke-web-design/).

Our bed already says this without their jargon. Stay on our sentences.

### Mute-first (this film is picture-first anyway)

- Instagram autoplay is **sound off** until you tap; session reset returns to silent. [PCMag UK](https://uk.pcmag.com/how-to/45683/how-to-turn-off-autoplay-videos).
- Meta’s own creative rule: **design for no sound**; unexpected loud feed ads make **80%** react negatively; captioned ads raised view time **~12%** in internal tests. [Meta: Capture Attention](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/updated-features-for-video-ads); [Meta: building video for mobile feed](https://en-gb.facebook.com/business/news/building-video-for-mobile-feed).
- Publisher-era “85% watched muted” is [Digiday 2016](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/) — old, still directionally true for feed; do not treat as a 2025 Meta KPI.
- Sharethrough (2021): **75% of mobile users keep the phone muted**. [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/365022/sharethrough-ups-engagement-with-dynamic-video-cap.html?edition=122974).
- Meta Instagram video ads: **under 15s** tends to win in feed; Stories under 10s; **design for sound-off**; add subtitles if there is speech. [Meta Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/188534925073536).
- TikTok/Reels culture is more sound-on than Facebook feed, but captions still travel. CHI 2024 on TikTok captioning: [ACM](https://dlnext.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642177). BBC News had to put **manual** captions back on Instagram because auto-captions failed. [Liam O’Dell, Feb 2024](https://liamodell.com/2024/02/12/bbc-news-instagram-captions-subtitles-bring-back-captions-campaign/).

**House lock already matches the science:** picture only; no baked film line; voice and captions later if wanted. If we add captions, burn them in ourselves. Do not trust platform auto-captions for the bed.

### How to show empty → first live **without saying iterative**

Do not say two versions. Say **first it is yours, then it can grow, and you say when**.

Picture grammar that reads as control, not as process:

1. **Empty live** = nothing public yet. Hold long enough that emptiness is a choice, not a loading spinner.
2. **Side key, not bars in the page** = they are leaving notes, not dragging a builder.
3. **First Push while live is still empty** = they decide the first public moment.
4. **Blip, not swipe** = it appears; we did not morph their site in public.
5. **Sandbox then pulls ahead** while live holds the first page = the host stays; the next work is beside it.
6. **Second Push** = they decide again. Same verb. Not “phase two”.

Spoken/caption grammar (when we add it): “You say when it goes live” covers both Pushes. Do not number them. Do not say “then we improve it”. The squares multiplying is the *page filling out*, which is what a first site *is*.
