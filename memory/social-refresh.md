## 1. Who this film is for

### The job, in one line

Someone already trading, already findable, already slightly ashamed or simply tired of what is up, who will not buy from a studio that talks down the wall they paid for last time.

### What they already have

This is not a first website. The UK Business Data Survey 2024 found **68% of UK businesses already have a website**; among small firms (10–49 staff) it is **93%**, micro **74%**, sole traders **65%**. Large and medium are effectively saturated (99% / 97%).

- https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/uk-business-data-survey-2024/uk-business-data-survey-2024

So the commercially real door for Design Lab North — identities, marketing strategies, brand redesigns and facelifts, websites and design for print — is mostly people with **something live**. Piece 3 (`new.html`) is the empty-live door. This film is the other one.

They may also have: a logo a family still likes; a WordPress or Wix or locked CMS they cannot move; a cousin, intern, or previous agency in the story; Google listings that still convert a bit; staff who update the shop hours; a marketing manager who has been asking for “a refresh” for two years and an MD who has been putting it off.

### Who commissions

Three layers, not one persona. The film talks to **you** (the person who can say yes). Different layers scroll different places; they share the same pride/shame knot.

**1. Owner-operators of established SMEs (primary).**

Family ownership is the UK default, not a niche. The Family Business Research Foundation, reading the Longitudinal Small Business Survey 2024 (fieldwork Oct 2024–May 2025): **73% of SME employers are family-owned**; **72% are both family-owned and family-managed**. Almost all family-owned firms are still run by the owning family. One per cent are family-owned and *not* actively managed by them.

- https://www.fbrf.org.uk/familysmes-2024
- https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/small-business-survey-2024-businesses-with-employees/longitudinal-small-business-survey-2024-sme-employers-businesses-with-1-to-249-employees

That means the person watching is often the person who commissioned the current site, signed off the last logo, or asked a relative to “just get something up”. They are not a procurement panel. They are also, often, a custodian. Family Business United’s 2025 survey: **82%** of current family-business leaders see themselves as stewards; **43%** expect a leadership transition within five years.

- https://www.familybusinessunited.com/post/family-business-growth-expectations-dip-and-leadership-transition-looms

A film that dunks the live site dunks their stewardship.

### Why they buy *now*

LSBS 2024: **40%** of SME employers cited **market competition** as a challenge; costs and tax were higher, but competition is the look-first lever. 69% already use digital tech to sell or manage; the site is not optional furniture.

Triggers that match 2024–25 UK casework (use as subtext, not on-screen checklist):

1. The street has moved and the live wall has not (Anglo 2016; PES slow/dated).
2. Competitors’ sites make the same offer look newer (Krystal Designs: “more than 3–4 years old and looks outdated compared to competitors”).
   - https://krystaldesigns.co.uk/resources/website-redesign-vs-revamp-which-does-your-business-need/

4. Leads down despite a good reputation (David Hill & Son).

6. A marketing manager has finally been allowed a budget, and needs a studio that will not humiliate the MD in the first meeting.

## 2. Beliefs / never-say

### Beliefs they already hold (do not argue; sit beside)

- **The work is real.** Vans, mills, clinics, parks, shops. The site is a lag, not the business.
- **What is up was a decision.** Someone they know made it. Money, favours, or both.
- **It still does a job.** Emails, hours, a phone number, the odd enquiry. Storyblok (Jan 2023) found **47% of UK businesses** had recently been embarrassed by their website in front of a stakeholder or customer — *and* **90%** said the site met all or most of their expectations. Shame and adequacy live together. If we only speak shame we miss the 90%. If we only speak adequacy they never call.
  - https://www.marketingtechnews.net/news/47-of-uk-businesses-are-embarrassed-by-their-website/
- **A rebrand can feel like a betrayal of heritage.** Thornley’s logo; Premier’s 65 years; Anglo’s Lancashire mill; Think Group keeping James Robinson Fibres / Texfelt in the story.
  - https://fishtankagency.com/work/think-group/
- **Live cannot go dark.** Green End Farm kept the live site up on a staging copy. That is our host loop, in the wild.
- **They have looked at competitors.** iwoca’s SME owner quote (Lunch'd): social is “an invaluable tool to keep an eye on what our competitors are doing.” Competition is already in their pocket. We do not need to lecture it. We need to show that we look too — then sit with *their* live wall, not a dunked screenshot of it.

### Beliefs we want them to leave with

- Design Lab North **look first** (market, conflict, competitor), then **sit with what is already live**.
- Facelift / rebuild are ordinary words, not a verdict on the last people.
- The next pass happens in a **sandbox**. Live stays until **you** say move.
- You leave a note. We take it into a human-led build. You choose when it is pushed.

### Never-say (commercial, not just manners)

| Never | Why it kills the sale |
|---|---|
| Dated, tired, embarrassing, amateur, cowboy, cousin, “Wix disaster”, “looks like 2012” | Names the shame they already feel. Closes the door. Insults the last maker. |
| Before / after as ugly → pretty | Same dunk, with a wipe. Generic agency glow-up. |
| “We will fix your site” as the first picture | Makes the live wall the problem. Our lead is the *market*, not their wallpaper. |
| “Notes magically improve a site” as the lead | That is the host-loop misread. Loop.html is the tail. |
| Iterative | Locked. The three notes *are* the engagement; do not print the word. |
| GBP, packages, retainers, “from £…” | Locked. No pounds on the public wall. |
| Cursor, the desk, AI, “agents” | Locked. Public word is builder / sandbox. |
| Invented awards, invented clients | Locked. Dave’s 2026-08-19 practice copy is already the public bio. Do not extend it here. |
| “Your current team failed” | A rebuild sits **with** who built what is live now. |
| “Coming soon” empty live | That is piece 3. This live is already up. |
| System, ecosystem, pipeline, methodology lecture | Piece 3 lock: no dense system language. This door is analytical, not academic. Do not lecture campus audits on camera. |
| Light/dark, pills, shadows, gradient orbs, sun/moon | Brand lock. Paper / Ink, chamfer. |
| They / their (about the viewer) | Ads say **you**. |
| Where the studio lives | Voice lock. |

### Why “we do not talk down what is up now” is commercially necessary

1. **The buyer built it, paid for it, or loves the person who did.** Family-managed = the MD is the brand. Dunking the site dunks them.
2. **Stewardship.** 82% of family leaders in the 2025 FBU survey see themselves as custodians. The live site is part of what they are keeping safe. Offer a next pass beside it; do not offer a funeral.
3. **The 47% / 90% split.** Embarrassment is real and not the whole story. A studio that only speaks embarrassment sounds like every other rebuild ad. A studio that sits with what is live, having looked at the market, is rare.
4. **Staff and last agencies stay in the room.** Marketing managers cannot forward a dunk to the MD. Previous designers may still freelance for them. Cousins come to Christmas.
5. **Live must keep trading.** If the picture implies we take the site down, or that it is unfit to be seen, we are asking them to halt the shop. Our actual offer is the opposite: live stays; sandbox works; **you** choose when live moves.
6. **Heritage is equity.** Case after case in 2024–25 keeps the mark, the name, the mill, the parks. Facelift said plainly. Not a wipe.
7. **Competition is the safe conflict.** LSBS: 40% already feel market competition. Pointing at the *street* is analysis. Pointing at *their* homepage with a red ring is an insult.

Respect is not softness. It is how a rebuild gets signed.

## 4. How sandbox → live is the *second* beat, after looking

### The three-beat spine

```
LOOK (new pictures) → SIT (new pictures) → HOST LOOP (existing loop.html, tail)
market / conflict / competitor     live is already up, we sit     notes → Push → live blips
```

Campus already walks audits as Look / Sit / Write. Do **not** name those stages on the film. Do not lecture CHSE. The picture does Look then Sit; Write is the note in the sandbox, and it comes last.

### Why look must lead

If the first frame is two rooms and a caret, the brain reads piece 1: “they type notes and the site improves.” That is true of the host package and wrong as the *pitch* for a firm that already has a live wall. They do not need proof that a CMS can update. They need proof we can see the street they are on, without spitting on their shopfront.

Look-first in 3–5 seconds, without a lecture and without insulting the live wall:

| Second | What we show | What we never show |
|---|---|---|
| Market | A field of similar chamfered shopfronts / windows. Same family, different rhythms. A street, not a ranking. | Their URL. A red circle. “You.” A broken page. |
| Conflict | Two pressures meeting: what the street now asks versus what most of those windows still say. A gap. Two arrows. Not a fight. | A loser. A “vs” dunk. Their logo in the weak corner. |
| Competitor | Other windows given **equal weight**. One window is simply *ours to sit with* next, not the ugly one. | Side-by-side “theirs bad / ours good”. Named rivals. Screenshots of real firms. |

The analytical plates are **looking out**. The live wall is not on stage yet. That is how we avoid a 3-second insult.

Then **sit**: the live room from `loop.html` idle — already populated, grey bars, mark in place. We do not grey it out further. We do not crack it. We do not stamp DATED. We sit beside it. Sandbox empty. That is respect you can see.

Then **the host loop as tail**: three notes type and cement in colour; Push; live blips to match. Meaning: the next pass is beside what is up; you choose the move. Not: notes magically save an embarrassing site.

### How this picture differs from `new.html`

| | Piece 3 — new (`new.html`) | Piece 4 — refresh (this) |
|---|---|---|
| Live at start | **Empty.** Hidden until first Push. | **Already up.** Grey bars, mark, a working wall. |
| First three notes | Identity, one slate square, words. Building a first page. | Do not build a first page. Look is *outside* the rooms. |
| Side patch keys | Yes. Edit requests off to the side. | Not the lead. If they appear at all, only as a hint in a long 20s cut — default is **no**. |
| Squares 1→2→3 | Yes, after first live. Growing a site that did not exist. | No. That language says “you have nothing”. They have something. |
| Two Pushes | Yes. First go-live then a second. | **One Push** in the tail (the host loop). They are not launching from zero. |
| Clock | ~17s cycle, two publishes. | 15s or 20s film; look + sit + one sped host cycle. |
| Risk if mixed | Refresh viewers think we only do start-ups. | New viewers think they must already have a site. |

Do not mix the pitches. Do not play `new.html` under this voice bed.

### How this picture differs from `loop.html` alone

`loop.html` idle is already the right *sit*: live up, sandbox empty. If we ship that as the whole refresh ad, the lead becomes “leave three notes and live catches up.” True of hosting; false as the reason a family firm hires a facelift. Piece 1 is a shared mechanic. Piece 4 is a different door that *ends* on that mechanic.

### How this picture differs from a generic agency before / after

| Generic glow-up | This film |
|---|---|
| Ugly screenshot → pretty screenshot | No real client wall. Schematic only. |
| Wipe, blur, “modernise” | Live **blips** after Push. No swipe (already locked on piece 1). |
| The old site is the villain | The street is the subject. The live site is material we sit with. |
| Overnight transformation | Sandbox works while live stays. You choose when live moves. |
| Stock office, rounded glass, floating mockups | Thrown away on piece 1. Chamfered rooms, Paper/Ink. |
| Voice: “your website is costing you” | Voice: we look first; we do not talk down what is up now. |

A before/after can be cut from our tail by a careless editor (grey live → coloured live). That cut is forbidden as a *lead*. The colour change is a publish, not a verdict.

## 7. Voice / captions

We look first. Market, conflict, competitor. Then we sit with what you already have live. The sandbox takes the next pass. You choose when the live site moves. We do not talk down what is up now.

Spoken length: roughly 12–14 seconds at a calm UK pace. Fits 15s if the last line lands on the hold; fits 20s with air after Sit.

## 9. Risks

### A. Looking like we dunk on their site

**How it happens:** grey live versus colour sandbox; a crack overlay; a red ring; Ewan frowning at a laptop; “before” labelled on the idle live room; speeding the blip so it reads as a magic glow-up; using a real dated homepage.

**Why it is fatal:** family-managed buyers; the 90% who think the site is “fine enough”; marketing managers who cannot forward an insult; the last maker still in the story.

**Guardrails:** live idle is *complete*, not broken. Colour in the sandbox is a next pass, not a correction of a mistake. No labels Before/After. Sit comes after looking *out*. Voice says the respect line, and the picture has already obeyed it.

### B. Looking like a generic agency glow-up

**How it happens:** leading with the host loop; wipe transitions; mockups in glass; “modern, sleek, professional”; stock people pointing at screens; before/after in the thumbnail.

**Why it is fatal:** indistinguishable from every rebuild studio on Instagram; wastes the only offer that is ours (look first, sit with live, sandbox, you choose).

**Guardrails:** new analytical plates that pieces 1 and 3 do not have. Host loop **tail only**. Chamfer, Paper/Ink, schematic. Facelift/rebuild said plainly if said at all — not “elevate”.

### C. Looking like piece 3 (new customers)

Empty live, growing squares, two Pushes, side keys. Refresh viewers with a live shop will feel spoken to as if they have nothing. Mixed pitch, locked against.

### D. Looking like piece 1 only (host mechanic)

True but incomplete. They think we are a hosting animation. We are a design hub doing a facelift/rebuild with a host loop as the working method.

### E. Lecture

Three labelled plates plus VO defining market/conflict/competitor plus Ewan explaining audits. Death on Reels. Plates must read in one second each. No body copy on them.

### F. Photoreal / model drift

Piece 1 already generated beige offices and threw them away. Refresh plates are even more tempting for a model (“show a high street”). Keep in-house HTML/GIF for the graphic master. If a model is used later, first/last frame from *our* stills, motion prompt: graphic paper layout stays graphic.

### G. TikTok-shaped cut

Refresh buyers are not 16–24. A jump-cut meme version will chase the wrong age. Keep the sit.

### H. Using a real competitor or a real client live wall

Legal and ethical. Also a dunk if the “competitor” is prettier. Schematic only.

### I. Ewan as “the guy who will replace your designer”

On camera he is a **builder** standing with the method. Dave is the designer. Do not imply the last designer is out because a builder appeared.

### J. Captions clipping

Already learned. No in-frame film line. Captions in a later export pass.
