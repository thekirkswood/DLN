## A.P.E.S. as Dave wrote it (APES2)

**One line.** A thinking tool for crossing the gap between our default ways of thinking and what the situation actually requires. “Stop monkeying around and get on with the job.” We are evolved apes: analytical planning arrived late; emotional and social were already there.

**The problem it interrupts.** Most difficulty is not complexity. It is partial attention. Under pressure we reach for fluency. Efficiency becomes an illusion. A.P.E.S. widens the view just enough.

**It does not tell you what to think. It tells you where to look.**

You use it by choice, at a moment that matters. Silently or aloud. Seconds or a sitting. Nobody else needs to know.

**Four mindsets (lenses, not people)**

Never label a person as an Analytical thinker. That is a temporary state in a room.

| Letter | Name | Looks for | Fails when |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Analytical | Logic, evidence, structure, coherence | Looks correct on paper and hollow in use. Certainty before the other three have spoken |
| P | Practical | Feasibility, resources, timing, execution | Activity that looks like progress. Ships cleanly and never quite means anything |
| E | Emotional | Felt experience, motivation, trust, meaning | Persuasion dressed as care. Photographs well and no one believes |
| S | Social | Relationships, culture, communication, shared understanding | Private correctness that cannot travel. The room nods and the culture does not |

**Identity, persona, mask.** A.P.E.S. works with how thinking already moves. We don masks and inhabit personas. That is adaptation, not deception. Passionate reason and emotional practicality. Enquiry rather than certainty. Evidence without worship. Blood, sweat, and tears are admissible.

**Three frameworks named in the pamphlet**

1. Recognise mindsets. Recognise bias.
2. Question the illusion of completeness. Elevate the chance of 360 thinking.
3. Frame the task: define it and understand its context.

**Three kinds of task (all still walk all four)**

- **Creative** — generate range. An idea may start anywhere. It must still walk the other three. Ask what it does, says, looks like, behaves as.
- **Critical** — for and against, from each mindset. Taste is not the test.
- **Decision** — what if. Hold the choice across all four, not the loudest seat. Record why.
- **360** (campus name for “full”) — hold all four at once. Completeness is an illusion until you look.

**Working cycle**

Intent → Framing → Action → Turnaround → Landing, with Reflection consolidating after (it does not reopen the task).

On campus the named stages are: **Launch → Action → Turnaround → Reflection → Landing.**

- Launch: frame the task, name the kind of thinking, set limits. Do not solve it yet.
- Action: get ideas moving. Range first. An idea that stays in one mindset is unfinished.
- Turnaround: know when to stop generating. Insight, tension, or a real choice has appeared.
- Reflection: collect what emerged. Patterns, gaps, connections.
- Landing: return to delivery. Keep only what holds across all four.

**Bounded tasks.** Known context, defined time, clear purpose. Prevents premature closure and endless drift.

**It is a process of attention, not a method of production.** No prescribed outputs.

**15 combinations** exist in APES2 (p.37) as recognisable outcomes of mindset pairs/spreads. **Not yet listed room-by-room on campus.** That is a known thin spot.

**AI chapters in APES2 (held as sense, not as campus rooms)**

- Designing tasks for AI *before* execution
- Task drift, task chains, and human re-entry *during* execution
- Repair, accountability, and guardianship *after* breakage
- Where A.P.E.S. goes — and where it deliberately does not
- Failure modes: logo-only, all-in-one marketing pile, content mill, “I’ll just execute my great idea”, systems people dropping AI into a workflow
- A.P.E.S. at the task boundary
- Authority and the premature ending of thought

**Speed-thinking.** Once learned, it is useful under pressure. Broaden the moment without stopping the conversation. Often silent. Reframe the question (is this really a creative ask, or a decision in disguise?).

**Second nature.** The aim is not to be remembered as a framework. It is carried lightly.

**Scaffolding example.** Same person, same day, same site: practical on the roof, social with the contractor, emotional at home, a private identity crossing a public one. Life is continuous embodied negotiation, not clean context-switching.

## TaskEx, IDS, cards (the agentic paper)

The long paper is *A.P.E.S., TaskEx and Contextual Intelligence Frameworks for Deliberative Agentic Systems*.

**Premise.** Current AI completes tasks and optimises locally. Human project environments need layered reasoning, contextual interpretation, and consequence-sensitive decisions. Intentional latency: pause, model alternatives, then act.

**A.P.E.S. layer.** Deliberative cognition. Consequence before action. Recursive assessment. Hierarchies: execution → interpretation → strategic analysis → socio-emotional assessment → institutional consequence → adversarial review → reflective recommendation. Human complexity (ambiguity, contradiction, negotiation) must stay in the process.

**TaskEx layer.** Orchestration. How reasoning becomes work.

Campus loop (faculty, not a Swarm product page):

1. Decompose — clarify intent, define outcomes, identify the questions that matter
2. Plan pathways — options, sequence, who, with what
3. Orchestrate — execute, coordinate, track, hold dependencies
4. Re-plan — assess context, review feedback, adjust
5. Escalate / route — issues go to the right seat (agent, human, or system)
6. Memory — record decisions, outcomes, context so the next cycle is not blind

Hive plate 6 shows TaskEx coordinating Sector / Governance / User agents into a collective interpretation and a human outcome. That multi-agent picture stays Swarm.

**IDS layer.** Environmental and identity framing. Cards model performer identity, stakeholder sensitivity, local vs global, institutional psychology, strategic implication, social response. Not yet a campus avenue.

**Interrogation cycle (how an LLM should walk context)** — same letters, different job from the mindsets:

- **Analyse** — What is happening? What matters here?
- **Probe** — What do we need to know? Where is the uncertainty?
- **Evaluate** — Implications, risks, trade-offs
- **Synthesize** — The most coherent way forward

**Cards on campus** (keep questions honest until context is understood):

1. What is it — facts, unknowns, evidence
2. Where / who — broader context, stakeholders, environment
3. Why — forces, root causes, what is really driving this
4. What could go wrong — risks, change, vulnerabilities
5. What could we do — options, alternatives, resources
6. So what — impacts, who is affected, which outcomes matter

Hive plate 4 vs conventional AI: not Question → Recommendation → Transaction. Instead Question → Behavioural context → Emotional/cultural context → Scenario modelling → Reflective clarity → **Human decision remains sovereign.** Non-persuasive.
